01 / QUICK ID

Is it giant reed, common reed, or giant cutgrass?

Use this 3-way visual sort outdoors. The flower heads are clues; confirm with the leaf base or spikelet.

01

GIANT REED

Arundo donax
HABIT · Massive leafy, cane-like stand
LOOK FIRST

Broad, often drooping blades on robust jointed canes. Check for conspicuous leaf-base lobes clasping the stem.

INTRODUCED IN TEXAS
02

COMMON REED

Phragmites australis
HABIT · Tall wetland reed with a soft feathery plume
LOOK FIRST

Often a silky-looking, branched plume. Verify tiny silky rachilla hairs between florets.

LINEAGE NOT YET KNOWN
03

GIANT CUTGRASS

Zizaniopsis miliacea
HABIT · Tall coarse marsh grass with long arching leaves
LOOK FIRST

Coarse leaves with fine sawlike margins, a very open branching panicle, and a long papery inner ligule.

NATIVE IN BROADER REGION
FIELD PATHWhole stand→Leaf base→Flowering head→Spikelet if needed
Never ID from height or plume color alone.

02 / FIND THE STRUCTURE

The leaf collar is your best second clue

Follow one leaf blade down to its sheath. Expose the inside where they join — that is the ligule.

A

ARUNDO

Broad, rounded leaf-base lobes

The actual Arundo blade base and pale fringed collar
Look: Prominent ear-like flanges wrapping around the culm.
Do not use hair presence by itself.
P

PHRAGMITES

Short inner membrane with fine fibers

Actual Phragmites blade–sheath junction and hair fringe
Look: Fringed ligule; measure with a millimeter ruler.
Species identified ≠ lineage identified.
Z

ZIZANIOPSIS

Long, papery true ligule

NOT THE LIGULE · Visible outside node and sheath; inner ligule NOT visible
Look for: Inner ligule 6–20 mm long.
Our photograph does not expose it.

03 / REPRODUCTIVE CHARACTERS

Heads first. Spikelets to confirm.

Compare real panicles, then examine mature spikelets under a 10× hand lens or magnification.

Arundo: branching head, often held relatively upright
ARUNDO

Large, stiffly branched plume; form varies.

Phragmites: many-branched, often silky plume
PHRAGMITES

Silky, feathery flowering head; form varies.

Zizaniopsis: conspicuously loose branching head
ZIZANIOPSIS

Large open panicle; male and female spikelets.
