## LOOK HERE FIRST
1. Examine the node: Polygonaceae ocrea (papery stipular sheath), measure rim bristles.
2. Examine a rooted base for perennial rhizomes, especially P. hydropiperoides; do not infer duration from cut stems.
3. Compare complete flowering racemes. P. lapathifolia often nods, but posture overlaps.
4. Under a hand lens, inspect tepal veins, style number and punctae: P. lapathifolia has diagnostic anchor-forked outer-tepal veins; P. glabra typically two styles and biconvex achenes; P. hydropiperoides usually three styles and three-angled achenes, with 4–10 mm ocrea bristles.
5. Photograph mature achenes on both faces and edges with mm scale. P. bicornis usually has a central hump on one face; P. pensylvanica generally does not. Fruit size overlaps.
6. Distinguish P. bicornis heterostyly from P. pensylvanica homostyly by examining **multiple plants**: one flower alone cannot demonstrate population-level floral morphs.

## Accepted-name concept caveat
FNA and POWO recognize P. bicornis separately, but some regional sources synonymize it under P. pensylvanica. Preserve accepted-name authority and original observation; P. bicornis identity remains provisional pending flower-population and mature-fruit evidence. Historical Polygonum combinations are searchable aliases, not separate learner taxa.
