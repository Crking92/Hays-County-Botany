### Family and genus placement
Juncaceae (rushes) vs Cyperaceae (sedges) vs Poaceae (grasses): inspect an **individual flower**, not whether the stem is round. Rush flowers have six persistent tepals (two sets of three), typically three or six stamens, and a superior ovary developing a many-seeded capsule. Sedge flowers are highly reduced within spikelets and usually form a one-seeded achene; grass flowers sit inside lemma/palea and glumes and produce a caryopsis. Juncus typically has glabrous leaves with open sheaths; Luzula commonly has conspicuously hairy leaf margins and usually three seeds in its capsule. These are *typical*, not absolute, family/genus-wide rules.

## LOOK HERE FIRST — morphology-first, three-way key
1. **Follow the entire flowering stalk to see if flowers are in dense round heads (glomerules) or in a more open branching inflorescence.** If several large round heads, inspect **J. texanus** first; if several small open clusters, inspect **J. filipendulus**; if a terminal branched inflorescence of individual small flowers rather than discrete globular heads, inspect **J. dichotomus**. Branching alone is insufficient: follow multiple flowering branches.
2. **At 10–20× magnification count stamens on a fresh flower** (three in **J. filipendulus**, six in **J. dichotomus** and **J. texanus**, per FNA). Stamens can be shed after anthesis; old fruits cannot be counted as three-stamen evidence.
3. **Compare ripe capsule with persistent tepals using a mm ruler**, then examine leaf blade and sheath auricles. This is the final tie-breaker; don't rely on wet habitat or stem height alone.

| Character | J. dichotomus | J. filipendulus | J. texanus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habit and leaf blade | Perennial, basal narrow blades, nearly terete/channeled or flat; 0.7–1(–1.2) mm wide typically | Tufted perennial, flat 1–2.5 mm blades; compressed culm, often swollen at base | Rhizomatous perennial; terete 1–2.5 mm blades, cauline leaves 3–5 |
| Inflorescence | Terminal 5–100-flowered branched arrangement, congested to somewhat loose; no large 10–40-flowered spherical heads | Usually 2–5 (range 1–10) relatively open glomerules, each commonly 6–15 flowers | Terminal panicle with 2–16 spherical heads, each 10–40 flowers, head ~11–15 mm across |
| Stamens | **6** | **3** | **6** |
| Tepals | Usually 3.3–4.5 mm, outer/inner near equal | 3.5–5 mm, outer/inner near equal | Outer ~4–4.9 mm; inner ~3.9–5.4 mm |
| Capsule | Tan/brown, ellipsoid, usually 2.8–3.5 mm; one- or pseudo-three-chambered | Tan/reddish, obovoid, 2.6–3.2 mm, **shorter than tepals**, 3-locular | Chestnut, slender lanceolate, ~5.2–6.5 mm (occasionally 8), exserted beyond tepals; one-locular |
| Strongest proof | Flower arrangement + 6 stamens + short ellipsoid capsule | 3 stamens + open few-flowered clusters + flat leaves | Dense spherical heads + long protruding slender capsule |

**Overlap and caveats:** Tepal lengths overlap broadly. J. dichotomus and J. filipendulus capsules overlap in size; flower/cluster architecture and stamen number are stronger. J. dichotomus leaf blade can be flat, so a flat leaf alone is not proof of filipendulus. Color, wetness, culm height, and the word 'rush' are not species diagnoses. Confirm head measurements on mature, voucher-confirmed plants. The project should also compare **J. marginatus**, **J. interior**, **J. tenuis** and **J. nodosus** as appropriate, not assume only three Juncus exist locally.
