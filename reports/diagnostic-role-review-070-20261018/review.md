## What the images support — and what they do not

- **Simsia photo 05:** A full-size back-of-head view clearly shows numerous green hairy involucral bracts around the base of the yellow ray florets and a hairy stalk. This is stronger teaching evidence than a generic yellow-flower label, but does not independently certify species determination or microscopic size.
- **Simsia photo 02:** Source metadata says *all rights reserved / license not supplied by API*. Caption updated, but **no public reuse or rights clearance** is implied.
- **Rudbeckia:** Yellow rays and a raised dark disk are conspicuous in multiple pictures. Some hairs are visible on a flower stalk. Leaves needed for **rough-hairy vs clasping-leaf** distinctions are not adequately shown and must not be inferred.
- **Cirsium:** The purple flowerheads occur among mixed broad foliage. Not every neighboring leaf can safely be assumed to belong to the thistle. A visually apparent involucre cannot be measured accurately without a scale.
- **Solidago:** The yellow flowering image is visually useful, but mixed goldenrod foliage and low leafy rosettes do not prove the named species. The fine stem indumentum distinguishing this goldenrod from similar taxa remains unverified in these images.
- **External media:** The additional *Cirsium texanum* Wikimedia Commons photo and *Solidago juliae* iNaturalist image are URL-only entries. Their original roles, creators, sources and licenses were **preserved unchanged**, and they were labeled external/uninspected in the contact sheets. They are not part of the 15 inspected local images.
