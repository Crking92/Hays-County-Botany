# Hibiscus / Muenchhusia scientific review (2026-10-11)

Current POWO/WCVP accepted concepts:
- Hibiscus coccineus Walter → Muenchhusia coccinea (Walter) M.M.Hanes & R.L.Barrett (2025). POWO: introduced to Texas; NON_NATIVE_COMPARISON; not invasive by default.
- Hibiscus laevis All. → Muenchhusia laevis (All.) M.M.Hanes & R.L.Barrett (2025). Native Texas state-level.
- Hibiscus lasiocarpos Cav. → Muenchhusia moscheutos subsp. lasiocarpos (Cav.) M.M.Hanes & R.L.Barrett. Accepted subspecies, NOT independent species card.
- Hibiscus martianus Zucc. → accepted Hibiscus martianus; H. cardiophyllus synonym. Native Texas state-level.
- Hibiscus moscheutos L. → Muenchhusia moscheutos (L.) M.M.Hanes & R.L.Barrett (2025); accepted subspp. moscheutos and lasiocarpos. Native Texas state-level.

LOOK HERE FIRST:
1. M. coccinea: very deeply palmately 3–5-lobed, glabrous leaves with narrow lobes; FNA blade 10–19 × 13–25 cm; long narrow red petals support, but color alone not decisive.
2. M. laevis: glabrous hastate/halberd-shaped leaf, FNA 6–18 × 3–16 cm; calyx 2.5–3 cm; glabrous capsule 1.8–3 cm. Compare M. moscheutos, often hairy and broader leaved; FNA notes hybridization.
3. M. moscheutos: FNA blade 8–20 × 3–13 cm, unlobed or 3-lobed; calyx 1.5–4 cm; capsule 1.4–3.5 cm. For subsp. lasiocarpos: usually HAIRY capsule, CILIATE epicalyx bractlets, hairy upper leaves. Subsp. moscheutos usually glabrous capsule, nonciliate bractlets, glabrous upper leaves. Characters vary; verify together.
4. H. martianus: woody-based plant with cordate leaf and dense stellate abaxial hairs; smaller dry-site rose-mallow; compare wetland Muenchhusia using leaf-base and hair structure, not flower color.
