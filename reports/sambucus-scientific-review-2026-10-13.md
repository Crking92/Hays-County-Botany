## LOOK HERE FIRST
1. Opposite pinnately compound leaves with serrate leaflets identify the elderberry group; *Viburnum* has simple opposite leaves.
2. American elderberry terminal broad cyme/panicle usually has five primary rays; UT Austin describes heads 75–384 mm across.
3. Inspect individual flowers (UT Austin 5–6.3 mm across) and ripe drupes (4.3–5.2 × 5–6.2 mm) with 3–5 hard pyrenes.
4. Do NOT diagnose European *S. nigra* vs American *S. canadensis* from leaflet count (roughly 5–7 vs 5–11), flower-head shape, or dark fruit color: these overlap. Require provenance/voucher for uncertain European material.
5. *S. cerulea* often has powder-blue waxy mature fruits, but wax can rub off; not conclusive alone.
