LOOK HERE FIRST: photograph the ocrea and its marginal bristles at the node; dissect the perianth for glands and outer-tepal venation; inspect a mature achene for biconvex versus 3-angled shape and count styles. P. glabra has eciliate ocreae and biconvex achenes. P. hydropiperoides has glands concentrated near tepal bases and usually trigonous achenes. P. lapathifolia has anchor-shaped veins in outer tepals and often nodding inflorescences. P. punctata has perianth glands spread across tepals, interrupted inflorescences and mostly nonoverlapping ocreolae. P. setacea has bristly ocreae (often 6–12 mm marginal bristles), spreading hairs proximally, gland-free perianths and trigonous achenes. P. hydropiperoides vs P. setacea can overlap; require multiple characters. Flower color, leaf blotch and wet habitat are unreliable alone.
