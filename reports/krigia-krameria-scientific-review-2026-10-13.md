## Krigia: a high-value source-name normalization
- Source **Krigia caespitosa** (row 609) is the common alternate orthographic spelling of **Krigia cespitosa (Raf.) K.L.Chambers**, the accepted spelling used by POWO and FNA. Preserve the source spelling as searchable alias; route to one accepted learner concept, not a duplicate. Do not assume this is the same as *K. occidentalis*.
- **Krigia occidentalis Nutt.** (row 610) is accepted by POWO and FNA.
- Both are Texas-native at state scale. Strict three-target-county PASS not demonstrated: RANGE_UNRESOLVED. Learner admission is approved by source-pool membership; hand off accepted concepts as approved_pending_curation (with mapping of *caespitosa* → *cespitosa*).
- **LOOK HERE FIRST**: photograph mature cypsela + involucre; look at the phyllary midveins, then pappus, then branching architecture. *K. cespitosa*: midveins evident but **not strongly keeled**, fusiform cypsela broadest above middle, **no pappus**. *K. occidentalis*: midveins prominent, curved/keeled at bases; broadly obconic cypsela; pappus usually **five short scales (0.4–0.6 mm) + five bristles (1.2–2 mm)**; peduncles arise from basal rosette. The word **usually** matters: fragile pappus can be lost; absence of bristles on an old detached fruit is not decisive.
- **Local look-alikes**: *K. wrightii* may be a small leafy-branched annual with reduced/absent pappus; *K. virginica* bears reflexed mature phyllaries and longer bristles. Generic yellow ligulate heads, overall height and flowering season overlap; don't ID from head color alone.
- **Exact diagnostic photos**: paired intact mature heads in side view with arrows to phyllary keels; dissected ripe cypselae on millimeter grid, show pappus bristles/scales vs absence; complete plant showing basal rosette vs leafy stems; include *K. wrightii* look-alike comparison if verified.

## Krameria: inflorescence and stamens separate species
- **Krameria erecta Willd.** (row 607) and **Krameria lanceolata Torr.** (row 608) are separate POWO-accepted species, native in Texas at state scale. Strict target-zone 3-county PASS remains UNRESOLVED, even with a Hays source-pool record for *K. lanceolata*.
- **LOOK HERE FIRST**: trace entire flowering shoot. *K. erecta* is a mound-forming woody **erect shrub** with long and short shoots, **solitary axillary flowers on compressed short shoots**, and **unequal/didynamous stamens**. FNA reports leaf blades **0.5–15 × 0.5–2 mm**, sepals **8–10 mm**, and fruit spines **2–5 mm**. Its fruit is cordate with a prominent ridge on one face.
- *K. lanceolata* is a **spreading/decumbent herb** with long shoots, **terminal one-sided (secund) racemes**, **equal stamens**; FNA reports leaf blades **5–25 × 0.9–4 mm**, sepals **8–16 mm**, fruit spines **1.8–5.3 mm**. Fruit round to slightly cordate with a ridge on each face.
- **Unreliable characters**: magenta/pink sepal color is shared; leaf length overlaps 5–15 mm; fruit spine length overlaps 2–5 mm; hairiness and soil substrate overlap. Use shoot + inflorescence architecture first, stamens and fruit ridges as corroboration.
- **Realistic confusers**: *K. ramosissima* is another erect shrub with shorter fruit spines (0.5–1.5 mm in FNA), *K. bicolor* has reflexed sepals; verify regional presence before describing either as local.
- **Exact diagnostic photos**: paired whole plants and flowering branches with arrows to terminal raceme versus axillary short shoot; dissection of 4 stamens with same-scale equal versus unequal lengths; both faces of ripe fruit with ridge arrows and mm ruler; label the five petaloid sepals separately from the true petals/oil glands.
- The existing Krameriaceae family module already covers oil-petal and poricidal-anther family traits; do not duplicate it or mislabel the sepals as petals.
