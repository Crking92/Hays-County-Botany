## LOOK HERE FIRST
1. The white star is formed by involucral bracts around a dense head of sedge spikelets, not ordinary petals.
2. R. colorata generally has several long radiating bracts with conspicuous white bases on both surfaces. R. nivea has few, shorter, almost wholly green bracts, sometimes absent.
3. FNA describes colorata with slender elongate rhizomes; nivea generally tufted without them. Damaged bases are unreliable.
4. Mature achenes: FNA reports colorata fruit about 1.5–1.7 mm versus nivea about 0.8–1 mm; examine the tubercle/style base and record the measurement convention. Head color alone is unreliable.
