## LOOK HERE FIRST
1. At plant base: perennial P. pilosa (subsp. riparia) has persistent underground axis; P. drummondii and P. roemeriana are annual taprooted.
2. At several stem nodes: P. drummondii often has alternate upper leaves (opposite at low nodes); P. roemeriana and P. pilosa riparia have opposite-decussate leaves. This is a tendency, not a universal key.
3. Compare calyx and pedicel with millimeter scale. A. C. Gibson's Williamson County specimens: P. drummondii calyx 6.3–6.8 mm, pedicel 3–4 mm, conspicuous glandular hairs; P. roemeriana calyx commonly 13–14 mm (low extreme 7), pedicel 10–15 mm, long hirsute hairs; P. pilosa subsp. riparia calyx to 15 mm with awnlike lobe tips 1–2 mm, pedicel to 6 mm, glandular hairs.
4. Flower widths in those local descriptions: drummondii 16–18 mm; roemeriana typically 24–33 mm (low extreme 15); pilosa riparia about 20 mm. Measurements from local specimens are NOT worldwide species limits; they overlap.
5. Check capsules, not color: local roemeriana capsule about 4.5 mm and several seeds; drummondii about 3.5 mm and 1–3 seeds; pilosa riparia 3.5–4 mm and 1–3 seeds. Seed counts overlap.

Family: fused five-lobed corolla, five epipetalous stamens, superior ovary and capsule. Phlox differs from project Giliastrum and Ipomopsis in simple foliage and typical salverform flower, but these are genus-level tendencies. Local UT description of P. drummondii calls the ovary one-chambered whereas other two local descriptions call it three-chambered; mark exact locule-count teaching as unresolved pending floral dissection.
