## LOOK HERE FIRST: four common Cyperus
1. **Examine the mature spikelet's architecture.** If it is cylindrical/subcylindrical and its rachilla breaks into **one-fruited segments** consisting of scale + rachilla internode with corky wings clasping the achene, investigate **C. odoratus**. This segmentation is much more useful than rusty color.
2. **If the spikelets are flattened, examine the basal organs and floral scales.** A cormlike swollen culm base, **deciduous** scales typically 3.2–4.5 mm, and a narrowly oblong achene suggest **C. strigosus**. First-year plants can mimic other Cyperus; require mature structures.
3. **For tuber-bearing stoloniferous plants, compare stolon texture and mature floral scales.** **C. esculentus** has softer, spongier stolons (even when dried), tubers usually 6–11 mm, and typically yellowish-brown persistent scales 1.8–2.7 mm. **C. rotundus** has wiry, hard stolons, tubers commonly 3–8 mm, and often purplish/red-brown persistent scales 2.6–3.4 mm. These ranges and colors overlap: require a character combination.
