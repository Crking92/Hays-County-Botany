## Taxonomy
- *Centaurea americana* Nutt. is a synonym of accepted *Plectocephalus americanus* (Nutt.) D.Don (POWO). Preserve the source name as an alias; do not create a duplicate Centaurea species card. Texas-native at state scale; three-target-county certification not established here.
- *Centaurea cyanus* L. remains accepted; native to the Mediterranean, introduced to Texas. NON_NATIVE_COMPARISON.
- *Centaurea melitensis* L. remains accepted; native to Mediterranean/Macaronesia, introduced to Texas. NPSOT separately categorizes it as invasive; eligible for INVASIVE_COMPARISON if that sourced status is propagated.

## LOOK HERE FIRST
Look beneath the composite flower head at the involucre and phyllary appendages, not merely at the flower color. *C. cyanus* has phyllary margins/appendages with narrow dark or pale **fringed teeth** around 1 mm, rather than the prominent sharp spines of *C. melitensis*. *C. melitensis* has a spiny-fringed phyllary base and a terminal spine about **5–10 mm**, with heads about 10–15 mm high; its cauline leaves may run down the stem as wings. Flower color (blue cornflower vs yellow star-thistle) is supportive but cannot substitute for bract structure. *Plectocephalus americanus* belongs to a different accepted genus: review its own mature involucre and head structure from a verified voucher before inventing an exact numeric key. Do not equate all three source records to one Centaurea genus concept.
