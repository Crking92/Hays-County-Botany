# Euphorbiaceae family recognition upgrade

## Family door
Start with the reproductive architecture, not latex: tiny unisexual flowers plus a superior, commonly 3-carpellate ovary that usually matures into a 3-part capsule/schizocarp. The persistent central columella is a strong confirmation character.

## Local/project-pool genus doors
- **Euphorbia** — cyathium.
- **Croton** — stellate/lepidote hairs; often pistillate flowers below staminate flowers.
- **Acalypha** — enlarging pistillate bracts and often multifid/laciniate styles.
- **Tragia** — stinging hairs, usually much smaller flowers, no latex.
- **Cnidoscolus** — stinging hairs + white latex + often deeply palmately lobed leaves.
- **Stillingia** — basal pistillate flowers, staminate clusters above, and persistent gynobase after fruit dehiscence.

## Best lookalike splits
- **Phyllanthaceae** — historically part of broad Euphorbiaceae; ovule number is a strong technical separator.
- **Apocynaceae** — latex can overlap; reproductive architecture differs strongly.
- **Urticaceae** — stinging hairs can overlap with Tragia/Cnidoscolus; confirm with Euphorbiaceae fruit/flower architecture.

## Visual shot list
1. 3-lobed ovary/capsule with central columella.
2. Labeled Euphorbia cyathium.
