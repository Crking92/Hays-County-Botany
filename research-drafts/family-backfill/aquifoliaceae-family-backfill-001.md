# Aquifoliaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## Where to look first

Start with a **woody plant with alternate simple leaves**, then ask two questions:

1. **Deciduous or evergreen?**
2. What do the **leaf texture and margin** look like?

Then confirm the family with the reproductive structures:

- tiny axillary flowers
- plants usually **dioecious**
- **superior ovary**
- fleshy red **drupe**
- several hard **pyrenes** inside

The red holly “berry” is therefore not a true botanical berry.

## The local three-way split

### Ilex decidua — possumhaw
**Deciduous + larger, thinner crenate leaves + red/orange fruit on bare winter twigs.**

Possumhaw is the easiest family anchor in winter because the leaves disappear while the drupes persist along pale gray branches.

B017: **strict-range PASS — Hays, Travis, Williamson.** Species curation still awaits explicit approval.

### Ilex vomitoria — yaupon
**Evergreen + tiny leathery leaves + fine rounded crenate teeth.**

Yaupon is usually a multi-stem shrub or small tree with pale gray bark and persistent red fruit on female plants.

B017: **strict-range PASS — Hays, Travis, Williamson.** Texas native material corresponds to *Ilex vomitoria* subsp. *vomitoria*. Species curation still awaits explicit approval.

### Ilex opaca — American holly
**Evergreen tree + large thick leaves + stiff sharp spine-tipped teeth.**

This one remains useful because it is common in the nursery trade and visually screams “holly,” but B017 found **zero project target-zone counties** under the strict native rule.

Status stays: **approved labeled comparison; target-zone FAIL; exclude from native totals.**

## Male vs female flowers

Aquifoliaceae is a really good family for teaching **dioecy**.

### Staminate / male flower
Look for:
- functional stamens/anthers
- reduced central **pistillode**
- no normal fruit production

### Pistillate / female flower
Look for:
- broad superior ovary
- broad/discoid stigma
- reduced **staminodes**
- later fruit production

A strong visual card should show these side by side with arrows pointing to the working versus reduced reproductive organs.

## Fruit lesson

The fruit should get its own anatomy card:

**red drupe → fleshy mesocarp → several hard pyrenes**

Useful words:
- drupe
- pyrene
- endocarp
- persistent stigma

That is a better family character than fruit color alone because possumhaw, yaupon, and American holly can all carry red fruit.

## Important cautions

Do not teach:
- “holly = spiny leaves”
- “holly = evergreen”
- “red berries = holly”

Possumhaw is deciduous and non-spiny. Yaupon is evergreen but only finely crenate. American holly is the large stiffly spine-toothed comparison.

## Main confusers

### Ebenaceae
Persimmons also have alternate simple leaves and fleshy fruit, but *Diospyros* fruit has a conspicuous enlarged persistent calyx and much larger seeds.

### Sapotaceae
Look for **milky latex** and large glossy seeds. Ilex does not use that latex cue and instead has several hard pyrenes.

### Rhamnaceae
Often shows more evident stipules and strong basal/arcuate venation; Ilex gives you dioecious tiny flowers plus the multi-pyrene drupe.

## Best visual sequence

1. possumhaw bare winter fruiting twig
2. yaupon evergreen fruiting twig
3. three-leaf comparison
4. staminate flower
5. pistillate flower
6. whole drupe
7. dissected drupe + pyrenes
8. Aquifoliaceae vs Ebenaceae vs Sapotaceae
