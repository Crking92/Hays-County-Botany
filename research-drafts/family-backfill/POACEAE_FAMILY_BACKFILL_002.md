# Poaceae Family Backfill 002 — First Five Priority Grasses

## Andropogon gerardii — big bluestem
**Look here first:** 2–6 finger-like racemes at the top of the culm. Then bend one raceme and look for paired spikelets.

**Confirm:** paired sessile + pedicellate spikelets; fertile sessile spikelet awned. Vegetatively, Hill Country plants are tall with compressed sheaths and hairs near the ligule/blade base.

**Best confusion check:** compare against indiangrass, little bluestem and Bothriochloa.

---

## Aristida purpurea — purple threeawn
**Look here first:** count awns. One lemma → **three long awns**.

**Confirm:** narrow rolled basal leaves, very short ciliate ligule, no auricles, hairy collar margin.

**Best confusion check:** *Nassella leucotricha* has **one** conspicuous long twisted awn, not three.

---

## Eragrostis intermedia — plains lovegrass
**Look here first:** broad, extremely open diffuse panicle with thin spreading branches.

**Confirm:** tiny multi-flowered spikelets; extremely short ciliate ligule; long hairs around the collar/blade base.

**Do not stop at "airy lovegrass."** Local *Eragrostis* separation needs spikelet + collar details.

---

## Muhlenbergia lindheimeri — Lindheimer muhly
**Look here first:** giant dense bunch + tall stiff culms, then the conspicuous membranous ligule.

**Confirm:** dense feathery panicle; branches flowered nearly to base; one-flowered spikelets with subequal glumes.

**Best confusion check:** seep muhly is smaller and much more open/wispy; *M. ×involuta* can be intermediate.

---

## Panicum virgatum — switchgrass
**Look here first:** pull the leaf away from the culm. Find the **dense white hairy ligule/collar zone**.

**Confirm:** prominent pale/white blade midrib + large open pyramidal panicle + tall rhizomatous habit.

**Best confusion check:** indiangrass has prominent auricles; switchgrass is famous for that white hairy collar/ligule.
