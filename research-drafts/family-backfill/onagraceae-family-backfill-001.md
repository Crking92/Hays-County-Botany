# Onagraceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for the first family module; remaining work is visual polish.

## Where to look first

Do not start with flower color. Start **under the flower**.

The strongest local clue is an **inferior ovary**: the green ovary is below the sepals, petals and stamens, and the rest of the flower often rises above it on an obvious **hypanthium / floral tube**.

Then look for the repeated four-part pattern:
- usually **4 sepals**
- usually **4 free petals**
- commonly **8 stamens**, but sometimes 4
- usually a conspicuous **4-lobed stigma**

## The big learner trick: Onagraceae has two very different local looks

### Classic evening-primrose form
Examples: *Oenothera speciosa*, *O. triloba*, *O. macrocarpa*.

Usually large, radial, open 4-petaled flowers. The inferior ovary/floral tube and 4-lobed stigma are easy to see. Fruits are often many-seeded capsules.

### Former-*Gaura* form
Examples: *O. curtiflora*, *O. suffulta*, *O. lindheimeri*, *O. suffrutescens*.

Flowers are carried along spikes/racemes and may be small or strongly one-sided. Stamens are often long and exserted. Most importantly, many members of **Oenothera sect. Gaura have hard, few-seeded, indehiscent, nutlike capsules**.

That fruit difference is worth teaching early because it prevents us from overfitting the family to pink/yellow evening primroses.

## Best anchor plants

### *Oenothera speciosa*
Best classic family example:
**4 broad petals → 8 stamens → 4 stigma lobes → inferior ovary → 4-valved many-seeded capsule.**

### *Oenothera triloba*
Fantastic anatomy card. The tiny flat rosette produces a huge yellow flower on a floral tube that can approach 10 cm. It also gives us an important exception: **4 stamens rather than 8**.

### *Oenothera suffulta*
Best fruit lesson for the old *Gaura* lineage:
white flower + crimson anthers + inferior ovary + **short hard indehiscent 4-lobed fruit**.

### *Oenothera curtiflora*
Shows that Onagraceae flowers do not need to be huge. Its flowers can be only about 3 mm across, but a close view still shows the hypanthium, inferior ovary, four petals, eight stamens and four stigma lobes.

### *Oenothera lindheimeri*
Best symmetry lesson. The petals are held mostly to one side, giving a strongly **zygomorphic** flower rather than the radial primrose look.

**Range guardrail:** its existing project status remains the user-approved range exception; the family module must not imply that Central Texas occurrences prove native target-zone status.

## Main look-alikes

**Lythraceae:** can also have a hypanthium and 4-part flowers, but its ovary is usually **superior**. The ovary position is the best split.

**Cistaceae:** typically 5 petals + many stamens + a **superior ovary**. Again, look below the petals rather than at color.

## Most useful training sequence

1. Side view of a flower: label **inferior ovary**.
2. Label the **hypanthium/floral tube** above it.
3. Flower front: count 4 petals.
4. Point to the 4 stigma lobes.
5. Compare radial *O. speciosa* with one-sided *O. lindheimeri*.
6. Compare *O. speciosa* opening capsule with the hard indehiscent *O. suffulta* fruit.
7. Finish with *O. triloba* as the “rules have exceptions” card: four stamens and an enormous square floral tube.
