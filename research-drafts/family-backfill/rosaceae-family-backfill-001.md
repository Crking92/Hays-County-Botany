**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## The family lesson

Rosaceae is too diverse to learn as “plums and roses.”

Start with:

**stipules + hypanthium + usually 5 petals + often lots of stamens**

Then ask the most important question:

# What kind of fruit does it make?

That splits our local learner beautifully.

## Four fruit architectures

### Prunus — drupe
One carpel becomes one fleshy fruit around one hard **stone**.

Think:
**Mexican plum / creek plum / black cherry**

### Rubus — aggregate drupelets
Many free carpels each become one tiny fleshy **drupelet**.

Put them together and you get a dewberry/blackberry-type aggregate fruit.

### Geum — aggregate achenes
Many free carpels each become a dry **achene**.

In white avens, the persistent style becomes jointed/hooked, producing the burr-like mature fruit.

### Malus / Crataegus — pome
The ovary is inferior and fused/adnate with the hypanthium/receptacle.

The fleshy outside is not simply an enlarged ovary wall like a plum.

That gives us the apple/crabapple/hawthorn **pome**.

## The hypanthium

This is the structural thread tying a lot of Rosaceae together.

Think of it as the flower cup surrounding or fused to the reproductive center.

But its relationship to the ovary changes.

### Prunus
Shallow cup around a **superior single ovary**.

### Rubus / Geum
Many separate carpels associated with the receptacle/hypanthium.

### Malus / Crataegus
Hypanthium/receptacle becomes adnate to an **inferior ovary** and participates in the pome.

So:

**hypanthium does NOT automatically mean inferior ovary.**

## Prunus mexicana — Mexican plum

Our strongest local family anchor.

Look for:
- flowers before leaf-out
- alternate serrate leaves
- petiole glands
- paired stipules when young
- 5 sepals
- 5 white petals
- many stamens
- cup-shaped hypanthium
- one ovary
- one-stoned drupe

FNA describes the fruit as a 15–30 mm purple-red to dark-blue drupe with a strongly flattened stone.

## Rubus trivialis — southern dewberry

Our aggregate-drupelet anchor.

Look for:
- trailing canes
- recurved prickles
- stipulate compound leaves
- 5 petals
- many stamens
- many free carpels
- black aggregate fruit

Each little bump on the dewberry is a **drupelet**.

FNA says R. trivialis fruit commonly contains about 10–50 strongly coherent drupelets.

Another useful fruit distinction:

the central **torus/receptacle stays attached** when southern dewberry is picked.

## Geum canadense — white avens

Our aggregate-achene anchor.

Look for:
- herbaceous habit
- stipulate leaves
- 5 white petals
- many stamens
- many free carpels

After flowering those carpels become many dry achenes.

FNA describes the mature styles as jointed, with a persistent segment ending in a **hook**.

That gives the mature fruit its snaggy/burr-like form.

## Pome lesson

### Crataegus viridis — green hawthorn
Useful family comparison:
- thorny tree/shrub
- stipulate serrate/lobed leaves
- many-stamen flowers
- small red/orange **pomes**
- several hard pyrenes inside

Keep its project target-zone status pending dedicated range review.

### Malus ioensis
Our Master Plant Pool row says:

**Malus ioensis var. texana — Blanco Crabapple**

Current POWO treats **var. texana as a synonym within accepted Malus ioensis**.

So the source name stays for provenance, while the learner uses current **Malus ioensis** taxonomy.

This gives us the classic apple-style pome and core comparison against Prunus.

## Prunus serotina var. eximia

One taxonomy item was worth confirming.

Current POWO **does accept Prunus serotina var. eximia** as a Texas-native variety.

So the existing curated Escarpment Black Cherry concept remains valid.

FNA uses a somewhat broader treatment under P. serotina, so we keep that source-treatment difference visible rather than flattening it.

## Main confusers

### Ranunculaceae
Can also have lots of stamens and free carpels.

But Rosaceae usually brings:
- stipules
- hypanthium

Ranunculaceae generally does not.

### Fabaceae
Can have stipules and compound leaves.

But the fruit settles it:

**Fabaceae = legume pod**

**Rosaceae = drupe / pome / achene aggregate / drupelet aggregate etc.**
