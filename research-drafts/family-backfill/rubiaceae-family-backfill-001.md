# Rubiaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## Where to look first

Don't start with the flower.

Start **at the node**:

**opposite or whorled simple leaves + something sitting between the petioles**

That “something” is the **interpetiolar stipule**, one of the best Rubiaceae characters.

Then confirm:

- fused corolla
- usually 4 or 5 lobes
- stamens matching the number of corolla lobes
- **inferior ovary**

## Buttonbush — Cephalanthus occidentalis

This is our best woody Rubiaceae anchor.

Look for:
- wetland/riparian shrub
- opposite or whorled leaves
- triangular stipules between the petioles
- round white flower heads
- long projecting styles producing the pincushion look
- inferior ovaries
- spherical fruiting heads

The flower ball is not one flower.

It is a dense head containing many tiny tubular flowers.

Likewise, the brown mature ball is a whole **fruiting head**, not one fruit.

## The Galium trick

Bedstraws look as if they have lots of leaves in a whorl.

But Rubiaceae is playing a morphological trick:

some of those leaf-like structures are **enlarged stipules**.

That gives us an excellent learner card:

### Cephalanthus
obvious little stipule between two leaves

### Galium
stipules become so leaf-like that they help form the apparent whorl

This may be the best way to make the term **stipule** stick.

## Cleavers — Galium aparine

Keep this plant in the learner because it is everywhere locally and extremely useful for ID practice.

But under current POWO, **G. aparine is native to Macaronesia–temperate Eurasia**, not North America.

So it is now labeled:

**INTRODUCED LOCAL COMPARISON — exclude from native metrics**

Look for:
- weak trailing annual
- 4-angled stem
- apparent whorls of 6–8
- backward-facing hooked prickles
- tiny white 4-part flowers
- paired hooked burr-like fruits

Everything sticks to you because those little hooks grab clothing and fur.

## Texas bedstraw — Galium texense

A native small-bedstraw anchor.

Look for:
- spreading annual
- 4-sided hairy stems
- whorls of 4
- tiny ovate leaf-like units
- tiny yellow-green 4-part flowers
- hooked bristles on the mature fruit

## Southwestern bedstraw — Galium virgatum

Also a useful native comparison.

Look for:
- small upright/ascending annual
- whorls of 4
- very tiny elliptic-oblong leaf-like units
- stems generally lacking cleavers-style hooks

Current POWO accepts the species and lists Texas in its native Central/SE U.S. range.

## Inferior ovary

This is the reproductive family check.

The corolla appears to sit **above** the ovary because the ovary is inferior.

That helps separate Rubiaceae from several opposite-leaved families.

## Main confusers

### Oleaceae
Both can have opposite woody leaves.

But:
- Rubiaceae = **interpetiolar stipules + inferior ovary**
- Oleaceae = no stipules + superior ovary

### Verbenaceae
Can have opposite leaves and 4-angled stems.

But:
- no defining interpetiolar stipules
- superior ovary

### Lamiaceae
Especially confusing with herbaceous bedstraws.

But:
- no interpetiolar stipules
- superior ovary split into four lobes
- fruit commonly 4 nutlets
