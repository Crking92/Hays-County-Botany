# Sapindaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## Where to look first

Start with the **fruit** and then check leaf arrangement:

- **Acer** — opposite leaves + paired winged samaras
- **Sapindus** — alternate pinnate leaves + translucent amber/orange soapberry drupes
- **Ungnadia** — alternate pinnate leaves + rough 3-lobed, 3-valved woody capsule

Modern Sapindaceae includes maples, which older sources may still separate as Aceraceae.

## Family characters

- woody trees/shrubs in our learner
- stipules usually absent
- leaves may be opposite or alternate, simple or compound
- flowers often 4–5-part and functionally unisexual
- floral/nectar disk commonly present
- stamens often 8
- ovary superior, commonly 2–3 carpels
- fruit may be samara, drupe, or capsule

## Acer negundo — boxelder

This is the great trick plant: it looks ash-like because its leaves are opposite and odd-pinnately compound, usually with 3 or 5 leaflets.

The fruit solves it:

**paired samaras = Acer**

That makes a useful crew comparison:
- boxelder/maple — paired samaras
- ash — single samaras

## Sapindus drummondii — western soapberry

Current POWO accepts **Sapindus drummondii**. Older sources often use **Sapindus saponaria var. drummondii**, which is now a synonym.

Look for:
- alternate pinnate leaves
- small flowers in panicles
- translucent yellow-orange to brown soapberries
- hard dark seed visible through the mature fruit

The plain *Sapindus saponaria* Hays-local source row is held for taxonomy/range review because current POWO treats it as a separate species and does not list Texas in its native range.

## Ungnadia speciosa — Mexican buckeye

Look for:
- alternate pinnately compound leaves
- rosy-pink flowers before or with leaf-out
- rough pear-shaped 3-lobed capsule
- capsule splitting into 3 valves
- glossy dark buckeye-like seeds

## Best visual lesson

Put the three fruits side by side:

**Acer** — paired samaras  
**Sapindus** — translucent drupe  
**Ungnadia** — 3-valved woody capsule

That single card should be one of the strongest family-recognition visuals in the project.
