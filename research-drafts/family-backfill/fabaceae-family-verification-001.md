## First rule: find the fruit

Fabaceae has extremely diverse flowers and leaves.

The best structural thread is:

**one superior carpel → one-chambered ovary → legume / modified legume fruit**

A classic bean pod is only one expression of that fruit.

Legumes can also be:
- indehiscent
- one-seeded
- flattened
- coiled
- winged
- fleshy
- or broken into one-seeded segments

So:

**fruit first, flower architecture second.**

## Modern taxonomy correction

Do not teach:

**Papilionoideae / Caesalpinioideae / Mimosoideae = the three legume subfamilies**

That is outdated.

Modern legume classification recognizes **six subfamilies**:

- Cercidoideae
- Detarioideae
- Duparquetioideae
- Dialioideae
- Caesalpinioideae
- Papilionoideae

The old **Mimosoideae** is now treated as the **mimosoid clade nested within Caesalpinioideae**.

We can absolutely keep saying:

- papilionoid flower
- caesalpinioid-like flower
- mimosoid flower

because those are visually useful architecture terms.

Just do not present them as the old three formal subfamilies.

## Papilionoid flower

The classic pea-flower lesson:

- **banner / standard**
- 2 **wings**
- 2 **keel** petals
- usually strong bilateral symmetry
- stamens commonly around 10 and often fused
- one superior carpel

### Lupinus texensis
An excellent local card:

- palmately compound leaves
- stipules
- banner + wings + fused keel
- 10 stamens fused into one column in the UT Williamson treatment
- superior 1-chambered ovary
- dehiscent pod whose valves separate and twist

## Cercis — don't expect a pea flower

Texas redbud is one of our best family exceptions.

It has:

- **simple heart-shaped leaves**
- flowers clustered on old woody twigs/short shoots
- 5 unequal free petals
- keel petals that are **not fused**
- 10 **free** stamens
- flattened legume

The pod makes the family relationship much clearer than the flower.

## Mimosoid architecture

Joey already gives us exceptional exact media for:

- **Senegalia greggii** — pod valves, seeds, bipinnate leaves
- **Vachellia constricta** — paired spines, bipinnate leaves and globose heads

These are now a family-breadth card.

Teach:

- tiny radial flowers
- usually bipinnate leaves
- flower heads/spikes
- lots of long conspicuous stamens
- legume fruit

But label it:

**MIMOSOID CLADE — Caesalpinioideae**

not “subfamily Mimosoideae.”

## Amorpha — the one-petal legume

This is a fantastic exception.

Amorpha belongs to the papilionoid lineage, but its flower has:

- **one standard/banner petal**
- no wings
- no keel
- 10 conspicuous stamens

Then it makes a little indehiscent legume.

So banner + wings + keel cannot be a family requirement.

## Dalea

Dalea is another good anti-bean-pod lesson.

Its fruit can be:

- tiny
- indehiscent
- one-seeded
- retained/shed with the persistent calyx

That is still a legume.

## Leaf architecture

Do not teach Fabaceae as simply “pinnate leaves.”

Our learner should compare:

- **Cercis** — simple
- **Lupinus** — palmately compound
- **Dalea / Amorpha / Sophora** — pinnate
- **mimosoid clade** — often bipinnate

Then add:

- stipules
- pulvini

as supporting clues.

## Main confusers

### Polygalaceae
Can look pea-flowered.

But milkworts usually give:
- simple leaves
- no stipules
- highly modified sepals/petals
- capsule rather than a one-carpel legume

### Rosaceae
Can also have stipules and compound leaves.

But:
- Rosaceae → hypanthium + Rosaceae fruit types
- Fabaceae → one superior carpel + legume
