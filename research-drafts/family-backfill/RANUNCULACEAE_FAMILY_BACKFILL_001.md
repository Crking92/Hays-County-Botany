# Ranunculaceae Family Backfill 001

## Where should I look first?

**Go to the flower center.** Look for **numerous free stamens + several/many mostly free carpels on a superior ovary/receptacle**.

Do not identify the family by flower color or "buttercup gestalt."

## The Central Texas trick

Ranunculaceae can look wildly different locally:

- **Aquilegia canadensis** — 5 sepals + 5 nectar-spurred petals + follicles.
- **Clematis** — petals absent; the showy parts are sepals; fruit is an aggregate of achenes.

That variation itself is worth learning.

## Ranunculaceae vs Rosaceae

Both can have many stamens and multiple carpels.

**Break the tie at the flower base:**
- Ranunculaceae: generally hypogynous, free stamens/carpels directly on the receptacle, usually no stipules.
- Rosaceae: commonly a conspicuous hypanthium; stipules are common.

## Priority species

### Aquilegia canadensis
**Look first:** the five long nectar spurs behind the flower.

Confirm five outer petaloid sepals, five spurred petals, many free stamens, and a cluster of follicles.

### Clematis drummondii
**Look first:** open 4-sepal flower vs huge silky fruiting head.

Petals are absent. Local descriptions show separate staminate and pistillate plants/flowers, followed on female plants by masses of plumose achenes.

### Clematis texensis
**Look first:** side view of the nodding scarlet leatherflower.

Four thick petaloid sepals make the urn, with sharply recurved tips.

## Important comparison
**C. texensis vs C. pitcheri:** use sepal margin/tip shape and flower architecture, not just red vs purple.
