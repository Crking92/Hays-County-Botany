# Commelinaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## Where to look first

When there are no flowers, start **at the node**:

- soft or somewhat succulent jointed stem
- conspicuous node/internode
- alternate parallel-veined leaf
- leaf base forming a sheath around the stem

When flowers are present, the family becomes much easier:

**3 sepals + 3 petals + superior ovary + usually 6 stamens or a mixture of stamens and staminodes + capsule.**

## The three local looks

### Tradescantia
The classic spiderwort form.

- flower usually radial in our main anchor
- 3 broad, similar blue/violet petals
- 6 fertile stamens
- often spectacular beadlike hairs on the filaments
- 3-valved capsule

**Tradescantia gigantea** is the best first family card.

### Commelina
The dayflower form.

- conspicuous folded green **spathe**
- flower strongly bilateral
- 2 enormous blue petals
- 1 tiny pale/white lower petal
- **3 fertile stamens + 3 staminodes**
- fruit develops largely hidden inside the spathe

**Commelina erecta** is our best card for teaching both **spathe** and **staminode**.

### Tinantia
The bridge between the other two.

- bilateral flower like Commelina
- 2 large blue petals + a tiny white lower petal
- no Commelina-style folded pocket
- **all 6 stamens are fertile**, but they are strongly different from one another
- several filaments have dense beadlike hairs, producing the fuzzy-face look
- 3-valved capsule hidden by persistent sepals

**Tinantia anomala** gives us one of the best stamen-comparison flowers in the entire learner.

## A killer stamen comparison

Put these three flowers side by side:

**Tradescantia gigantea**
6 fertile stamens, all broadly similar; hairy filaments

**Commelina erecta**
3 fertile stamens + 3 sterile staminodes/antherodes; filaments glabrous

**Tinantia anomala**
6 fertile stamens, but strongly heteromorphic; several with conspicuous fuzzy filament hairs

That is an excellent way to teach the difference between a **stamen** and a **staminode**.

## Vegetative ID

This family can fool you into thinking “grass.”

Look for:
- succulent/jointed stem
- obvious nodes
- leaf base wrapping the stem as a sheath
- adventitious roots at nodes in some species
- no typical grass ligule/spikelet architecture

## Main confusers

### Poaceae
Grass sheaths/parallel leaves can look similar vegetatively, but grasses give you the ligule + spikelet system rather than showy 3-sepal/3-petal flowers.

### Iridaceae
Irises and blue-eyed grasses have a monocot 3+3 plan too, but their six perianth parts are tepals rather than clearly different sepals and petals, and the ovary is generally inferior.

## Conservation guardrail

*Tradescantia pedicellata* deserves special handling. TPWD tracks it as a Texas endemic Species of Greatest Conservation Need, associated with the Llano Uplift. Do not infer its distribution from other spiderwort species.
