# Platanaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## This one is easier than it looks

For American sycamore, start with three things:

**mottled exfoliating bark + huge alternate palmate leaf + hanging fuzzy fruit ball**

Then learn the killer twig character:

> The swollen petiole base covers the axillary bud while the leaf is attached.

When the leaf falls, the bud is exposed.

That is an excellent winter/sterile-season ID character.

## Family framework

Current POWO treats Platanaceae as a monogeneric family: **Platanus**.

Useful characters:

- deciduous tree
- alternate simple palmately lobed leaves
- leaf-like stipules partly sheathing the twig
- ring-like stipule scar
- swollen/hollow petiole base covering the axillary bud
- no true terminal bud
- separate male and female spherical flower heads on the same tree
- spherical fruiting heads packed with many achenes
- exfoliating patchwork bark

## Platanus occidentalis — American sycamore

This is our sole local family anchor.

### Look here first
The bark is often enough to stop you:

- lower trunk can be dark/scaly
- upper trunk and large limbs shed plates
- fresh exposed bark becomes chalky white, cream, tan, gray or greenish

Then check the leaf:

- alternate
- huge
- usually 3–5 lobed
- palmately veined
- coarsely toothed
- stipulate

## Hidden-bud lesson

This deserves its own learner card.

1. Photograph a leaf still attached.
2. Label the swollen petiole base.
3. Show that the axillary bud sits inside/under that base.
4. Remove or show a fallen leaf.
5. The conical bud is now visible.
6. Point out the encircling scar.

That one sequence will separate sycamore from a lot of lookalikes.

## Stipules

Platanus also gives us a good **stipule** lesson.

The stipules are leaf-like and partly sheath the twig.

When they fall, they leave a scar that runs around the twig.

Useful words:
- stipule
- sheathing
- stipular scar
- node

## Flowers and fruit

The tree is **monoecious**.

Male and female flowers are separate, but occur on the same tree.

Both are packed into spherical heads.

The brown winter “ball” is not one fruit.

It is a **fruiting head containing many individual achenes**.

Each achene has basal hairs that help with wind dispersal.

## Main confusers

### Acer
Both can have large palmate leaves.

But:
- Acer = **opposite leaves**
- Platanus = **alternate leaves**
- Acer = paired samaras
- Platanus = spherical achene head

### Liquidambar
Both have alternate lobed leaves and hanging round structures.

But:
- Liquidambar leaf is more sharply star-shaped
- Liquidambar fruit ball is hard and spiny, made of capsules
- Platanus fruit head is fuzzy and breaks apart into hairy achenes
- Platanus has the hidden-bud petiole character
