# Oleaceae family backfill — local visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build, with the existing range holds on *Fraxinus berlandieriana* and *F. greggii* preserved.

## Where to look first

Start at the **node**. Local Oleaceae is woody and normally has **opposite or subopposite leaves/branching with no stipules**. Then split the two local genera by leaf and fruit:

- **Fraxinus (ash):** odd-pinnate compound leaf + **samara**
- **Forestiera:** simple leaf + **one-seeded drupe**

That is a better Central Texas teaching rule than “four petals + two stamens.” The family often has a 4-merous corolla and two stamens, but our local focal genera are frequently **apetalous**, and *Forestiera pubescens* is a useful exception to a rigid two-stamen rule.

## Core recognition characters

- Trees or shrubs.
- Leaves opposite/subopposite and **exstipulate**.
- Leaves simple or odd-pinnately compound.
- Flowers radial and often tiny; corolla may be absent in local *Fraxinus* and *Forestiera*.
- Ovary superior, usually 2-carpellate and 2-loculed.
- Fruit is the strongest local confirmation: **ash samara vs Forestiera drupe**.

## Best local visual sequence

1. Opposite node/branching and no stipule.
2. Ash odd-pinnate leaf: leaflet, petiolule, rachis.
3. Ash samara: label seed body and wing.
4. Texas ash vs green ash mature samara: show how far the wing runs down the seed body.
5. Forestiera simple opposite leaf.
6. Forestiera early apetalous flowers on bare twig.
7. Forestiera dark drupe.
8. Oleaceae node vs Rubiaceae node: **no stipule vs interpetiolar stipule**.

## Priority cards

### *Forestiera pubescens* — Downy Forestiera / stretchberry
Look first for opposite/subopposite simple leaves and elbow-like branch architecture. Flowers appear very early, lack a corolla, and the dark fruit is a drupe. The Williamson County treatment reports 4–6 stamens in staminate flowers, which is exactly why the learner should not memorize “Oleaceae always has two stamens.”

Specific study images are already linked for:
- opposite branching/foliage
- staminate apetalous flowers
- mature drupes

### *Fraxinus albicans* — Texas ash
Confirm ash with opposite branching + odd-pinnate leaves. Best species character when available: **samara wing terminal or only slightly decurrent**. Lower leaf surface/glaucousness and leaf-scar/bud form are secondary comparison characters.

Taxonomy note: current POWO accepts *F. albicans*; reusable Commons material may still be filed as the synonym *F. texensis*.

### *Fraxinus pennsylvanica* — Green ash
Confirm ash first. Then compare the fruit: the **wing runs distinctly farther down the seed body** than in Texas ash, and the samaras often form denser clusters. Leaf scars/bud position help when the tree is not fruiting.

### *Forestiera angustifolia* — Narrowleaf Forestiera / elbowbush
Dense opposite “elbow” branching + narrow leathery simple leaves. Tiny flowers lack petals; fruit is a small black one-seeded drupe. Texas A&M provides an excellent study reference, but a rights-safe living leaf+fruit close-up is still worth hunting.

## Main look-alikes

**Rubiaceae:** opposite leaves too, but typically has **interpetiolar stipules** and an **inferior ovary**.

**Bignoniaceae:** opposite leaves are common, but the showy bilateral trumpet flower + long capsule with winged seeds is a very different reproductive package.

**Lamiaceae / Verbenaceae:** opposite leaves can mislead. Use woody habit, lack of stipules, flower symmetry and—locally—the samara/drupaceous fruit.
