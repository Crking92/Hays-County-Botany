# Bignoniaceae vs Plantaginaceae vs Acanthaceae

## Don't identify these by "tubular flower"

All three can give you fused, bilateral flowers.

### Bignoniaceae
**Look for woody habit + long capsule + flat winged seeds.**

Typical local pattern:
- vine/tree/shrub
- opposite leaves, often compound
- 4 didynamous stamens + staminode
- long 2-valved capsule
- many flat winged seeds

### Plantaginaceae / Penstemon
**Look inside the flower for the beardtongue.**

Penstemon:
- herbaceous
- simple leaves
- 4 fertile stamens
- 1 sterile staminode
- ordinary many-seeded capsule

### Acanthaceae
**Open the capsule.**

The killer character is the **retinaculum**:
a woody hook/jaculator that cradles the seed and helps launch it when the capsule opens.

## Fast key

> **Winged seeds in long pod → Bignoniaceae**  
> **Beardtongue/staminode → Penstemon / Plantaginaceae**  
> **Retinacula in explosive capsule → Acanthaceae**

## Taxonomy cleanup
Current POWO accepts **Ruellia ciliatiflora** and treats source-pool names **R. nudiflora**, **R. nudiflora var. nudiflora**, and **R. occidentalis** as synonyms. Keep their source provenance, but do not create duplicate learners.
