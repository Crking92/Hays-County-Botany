## Where to look first

For our local mallow anchors:

- alternate/spiral, usually stipulate leaves
- often palmate venation
- stellate hairs
- usually a 5-part radial flower
- numerous stamens commonly fused into a **staminal column**
- superior ovary
- segmented dry or fleshy fruit

The staminal column is the quickest local cue.

## Broad-family caution

Broad Malvaceae also contains lineages historically treated as Tiliaceae, Sterculiaceae, Bombacaceae and others. Across that broader family, stamens can be free, fascicled or fused; petals can be reduced or absent; and fruit architecture is diverse.

So teach:

**staminal column = excellent local Malvoideae character**

not:

**every Malvaceae must look like Hibiscus.**

## Local anchors

### Malvaviscus arboreus var. drummondii
Our canonical Turk's-cap package is an excellent staminal-column lesson. The red petals stay tightly furled and the reproductive column projects well beyond them. It also gives us the fleshy-fruit exception.

### Callirhoe involucrata
Use this as the open-flower + dry-mericarps anchor. It makes the classic mallow floral construction easier to see than Turk's cap.

### Callirhoe leiocarpa
Preserve the no-epicalyx exception so the learner does not make an epicalyx mandatory.
