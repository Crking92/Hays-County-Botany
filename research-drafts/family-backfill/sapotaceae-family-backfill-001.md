# Sapotaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## Important project correction

The old audit action said to build this around **Sideroxylon celastrinum**.

But the live Master Plant Pool actually contains:

**Sideroxylon lanuginosum — gum bumelia**

with 17 wild observations plus Hays Local, NPSOT and Personal List support.

So the family module now follows the actual project plant.

S. celastrinum remains a valid Texas species and can become a comparison later, but it should not silently replace the live-pool anchor.

## Where to look first

Think:

**woody plant + alternate entire leaves + sticky/milky latex + tiny axillary flowers + fleshy dark fruit**

Then zoom into the flower.

Sideroxylon has a fantastic floral pattern:

- 5 corolla lobes
- 5 fertile stamens **opposite** those lobes
- 5 petaloid **staminodes** alternating between the stamens

That makes this one of our better families for teaching what a staminode actually is.

## Sideroxylon lanuginosum — gum bumelia

Look for:

- shrub or small/medium tree
- alternate simple entire leaves
- hairy/woolly lower leaf surfaces
- axillary clusters of tiny pale flowers
- branches sometimes ending in sharp spines
- purplish-black/black berries
- one disproportionately large glossy seed in the local Williamson treatment

### Important thorn warning

Thorns are useful when present.

But FNA describes the stems as **armed or unarmed**.

So:

**thorn present = good supporting clue**

**thorn absent ≠ not gum bumelia**

## Latex

Sapotaceae very often has sticky white latex.

This is a powerful family clue, but we do not need the crew cutting healthy trees every time.

Prefer:
- naturally broken twigs
- damaged tissue already present
- fallen/broken fruit
- incidental minimal exposure

Use latex as supporting evidence, not a destructive field test.

## Flower anatomy

This is the high-value macro:

**corolla lobe → fertile stamen directly opposite → petaloid staminode between adjacent stamens**

The Williamson County treatment gives:

- 5 sepals
- 5-lobed corolla
- 5 fertile stamens
- 5 staminodes
- superior 5-chambered ovary
- one ovule per chamber

## Fruit and seed

The mature local fruit is a small black berry.

Cut it open and the seed is enormous compared with the fruit:

- hard
- glossy
- dark
- conspicuous basal scar/hilum

That makes a great comparison against our last two woody-fruit families.

### Ebenaceae / Diospyros
- berry with several large seeds
- huge persistent calyx
- no classic Sapotaceae latex cue

### Aquifoliaceae / Ilex
- drupe
- several hard pyrenes
- no latex/staminode pattern

### Sapotaceae / Sideroxylon
- latex
- staminodes
- local fruit usually one large glossy seed

## Central Texas subspecies problem

We should **not** force a subspecies yet.

The UT Hays flora calls its local plant:

*Sideroxylon lanuginosum* subsp. **albicans**

The Williamson County treatment uses:

*Sideroxylon lanuginosum* subsp. **oblongifolium**

And the authorities do not line up perfectly:

- current POWO puts the old **albicans** name under subsp. **lanuginosum**
- FNA lists **albicans** in synonymy under subsp. **oblongifolium**

So the crew learner stays at:

**Sideroxylon lanuginosum**

until we run a dedicated Central Texas subspecies reconciliation.
