# Cannabaceae vs Ulmaceae — Hackberry vs Elm

## Where should I look first?
**Leaf base/venation → then fruit.**

### Celtis / Cannabaceae
- often asymmetric leaf base
- many species strongly 3-nerved near the base
- surface can feel rough/scabrous
- **fleshy drupe**

### Ulmus / Ulmaceae
- asymmetric/oblique leaf base
- pinnate venation
- serrate to doubly serrate margin
- **flat winged samara**

## Fastest tie-breaker
If the leaves look elm-like, look for fruit:
- berry-like drupe = hackberry
- papery winged samara = elm

## Local anchors
**Celtis laevigata** — sugar hackberry  
**Celtis reticulata** — netleaf hackberry  
**Ulmus crassifolia** — cedar elm

## Taxonomy note
Older Flora of North America treatments place *Celtis* in Ulmaceae. Current Kew/POWO classification places *Celtis* in **Cannabaceae**. The learner follows the current family placement.
