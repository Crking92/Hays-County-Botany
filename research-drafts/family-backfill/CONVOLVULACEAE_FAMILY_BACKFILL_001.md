# Convolvulaceae Family Backfill 001

## Where should I look first?

**Start behind the flower.**

Find **5 usually free, persistent sepals**, then look at the corolla:

**fused + 5-fold + pleated/plicate**.

Those pleats often remain visible as five bands or folds in an open morning-glory-type flower.

## Family sequence

1. Alternate leaves, usually no stipules.
2. Five free/persistent sepals.
3. Fused 5-part corolla, usually funnel/bell/tube.
4. Look for plicate folds / mid-petaline bands.
5. Superior ovary.
6. Usually a dry capsule with relatively few seeds.

## Do not define the family as "a morning-glory vine"

*Cuscuta* is inside Convolvulaceae and can be leafless and parasitic.

## Best confusion checks

### vs Apocynaceae
Convolvulaceae usually:
- alternate leaves
- free sepals
- pleated corolla
- capsule

Apocynaceae commonly:
- opposite/whorled leaves
- different reproductive architecture
- follicles/other fruit types

### vs Passifloraceae
Passiflora has:
- tendrils
- a corona
- reproductive column
- 3 prominent stigmas

### vs Solanaceae
Solanaceae usually has a **fused calyx**, while Convolvulaceae typically has **free overlapping sepals**.
