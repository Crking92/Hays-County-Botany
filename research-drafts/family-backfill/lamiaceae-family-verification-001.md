## Where to look first

Start at the node:

- leaves usually opposite/decussate
- stipules absent
- herbaceous stem often square
- foliage often aromatic

Then confirm reproductively:

- tubular, often bilabiate corolla
- 2 or 4 stamens
- superior ovary, often deeply 4-lobed
- style often gynobasic
- commonly 4 nutlets inside a persistent calyx

Those are the **common mint-family pattern**, not absolute rules.

## Important exceptions

### Salvia
Do **not** teach “mint family = 4 stamens.”

Salvia has **2 fertile stamens**. The elongated connective forms the famous lever mechanism that can place pollen onto a visiting pollinator.

This deserves its own animation/card.

### Scutellaria
The diagnostic feature is the **scutellum**:

a transverse shield-like appendage on the upper calyx lip.

That is a much better skullcap character than “opposite leaves and square stem.”

### Callicarpa americana
American beautyberry is the family-breadth card.

It is:
- woody
- opposite-leaved
- more radially flowered
- **drupaceous-fruited**

The purple fruit contains hard pyrenes rather than presenting the classic four dry nutlets.

So **four nutlets are common in Lamiaceae, not universal.**

### Warnockia scutellarioides
Our prairie-brazoria row is already using the current accepted genus.

Current POWO accepts **Warnockia scutellarioides** in Lamiaceae and treats **Brazoria scutellarioides** as a synonym.
