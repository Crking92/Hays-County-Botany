# Orobanchaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## The first misconception to kill

**Orobanchaceae does not mean pale, leafless parasite.**

Many of our local members are completely green **hemiparasites**. They photosynthesize normally but also form underground **haustoria** on host roots.

That means *Castilleja* and *Buchnera* can look like ordinary green wildflowers while still being parasitic.

Do not uproot plants to demonstrate the host connection. Teach haustoria with a diagram.

## Where to look first

For the local family lesson:

- simple leaves, no stipules
- bracted terminal spike/raceme
- fused tubular, usually bilateral corolla
- commonly 4 stamens
- superior ovary
- dry many-seeded capsule

Then ask whether you are looking at **Castilleja** or **Buchnera**.

## Castilleja — paintbrush

The bright color is the trick.

Much of what looks like the “flower” is actually **colored bracts** — modified leaves.

The actual flower is the narrower tubular structure emerging from among those bracts.

### Castilleja indivisa
Best first anchor:
- green annual hemiparasite
- alternate, simple, clasping leaves
- scarlet/red terminal bracts
- bilateral calyx + corolla
- 4 stamens
- superior 2-chambered ovary
- many-seeded capsule

This is an excellent **bract vs flower** card.

## Buchnera — bluehearts

This gives us a completely different-looking green hemiparasite.

### Buchnera americana
Look for:
- rough/hairy green stem
- **opposite** sessile leaves
- terminal spike
- deep purple tubular flowers
- 4 short included stamens
- oblong capsule with tiny seeds

The plant also has the classic Orobanchaceae tendency to **darken or blacken when bruised/dried**.

That blackening is useful supporting evidence but should not be our primary field key.

## Taxonomy cleanup

The source pool had been undercounting the family because **Buchnera was not mapped to Orobanchaceae**.

That is fixed.

We now have **2 genera / 4 source rows**.

Also corrected:

**Castilleja purpurea var. lindheimeri → Castilleja lindheimeri**

Current POWO/FNA accepts *C. lindheimeri* as a separate species and treats the old variety name as its synonym. Kew describes its range as the **Texas Edwards Plateau**.

*Castilleja purpurea* remains a separate accepted species.

## Hemiparasite vs holoparasite

### Hemiparasite
- green
- photosynthetic
- also taps host roots
- *Castilleja*, *Buchnera*

### Holoparasite
- little/no chlorophyll
- depends heavily on host
- often pale/yellow/brown
- classic broomrape appearance

A side-by-side card will stop the learner from thinking every broomrape-family plant has to look ghostly.

## Main confusers

### Plantaginaceae
Both can have:
- tubular bilateral flowers
- 4 stamens
- capsules

Our Penstemon framework often adds a conspicuous **staminode**. Orobanchaceae also has the crucial parasitic/hemiparasitic biology.

### Lamiaceae
Especially relevant for opposite-leaved Buchnera.

Mint family usually gives:
- square aromatic stem
- 4-lobed ovary
- 4 nutlets

Orobanchaceae gives:
- ordinary superior syncarpous ovary
- many-seeded capsule
