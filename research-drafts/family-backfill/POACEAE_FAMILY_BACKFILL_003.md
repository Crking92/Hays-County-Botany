# Poaceae Family Backfill 003 — Remaining Priority-1 Queue

## Important taxonomy correction: silver bluestem

The priority queue still contained **Bothriochloa laguroides**, but current POWO treatment separates the Texas plant:

- **Bothriochloa torreyana** = accepted, native Texas silver bluestem.
- **Bothriochloa laguroides subsp. torreyana** = synonym of *B. torreyana*.
- **Bothriochloa laguroides** sensu stricto = accepted species native from Mexico southward, not Texas.

Because *B. torreyana* is already a curated learner anchor, this queue item is treated as a **taxonomy resolution, not a second species card**.

## Nassella leucotricha — Texas wintergrass
**Look first:** one mature floret → **one very long twisted/bent awn**.

**Tie-breaker:** *Aristida* has three awns; *Nassella leucotricha* has one.

Also useful: open wispy panicle, glumes longer than the floret, short membranous ligule, often hairy nodes/sheaths.

## Sorghastrum nutans — Indiangrass
**Look first:** the collar region. Find the paired upright **rabbit-ear / gun-sight horns**.

**Tie-breaker:** switchgrass has a conspicuous white hairy ligule/collar instead.

Flowering plants confirm with a golden contracted panicle and hairy one-flowered awned spikelets.

## Sporobolus compositus — tall/composite dropseed
**Look first:** narrow spike-like contracted panicle, often partly swallowed by the upper sheath.

**Confirm:** minute ciliate ligule, one-flowered awnless spikelets, unequal glumes.

**Important:** the *S. compositus* complex is difficult in Central Texas. UT herbarium research documents real disagreement among determinations. Fine lemma surface characters and sometimes wet-pericarp behavior matter.

## Tripsacum dactyloides — eastern gamagrass
**Look first:** the thick flowering branch.

- **female hard bead-like joints below**
- **male spikelets/anthers above**

That reproductive layout, combined with a huge broad-leaved rhizomatous clump, is exceptionally distinctive.
