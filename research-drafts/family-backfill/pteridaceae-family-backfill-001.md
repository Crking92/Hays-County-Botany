# Pteridaceae family backfill — Central Texas visual learner

**Status:** ready for first family module build; visual polish pending.

## First rule: stop looking for flowers

This family is a great chance to teach the crew how to actually read a fern.

Start with:

**frond → stipe → rachis → pinna → pinnule → flip it over → sori**

The reproductive structures are on the underside.

## The family-level clue

For many of our local Pteridaceae:

- sori are **marginal or submarginal**
- there is **no true indusium**
- the leaf edge curls/recurves over the sori
- that rolled leaf tissue creates a **false indusium**

That is one of the most useful fern-family characters we can teach.

## True indusium vs false indusium

### True indusium
A separate flap/membrane covering the sorus.

### False indusium
The **leaf blade itself** rolls over the sori.

So on these ferns:

**rolled leaf edge ≠ separate membrane**

## Rough lipfern
### Source name: Cheilanthes horridula
### Current POWO: Hemionitis aspera

Look for:
- small tufted rock fern
- dark red-purple/black axes
- twice-divided frond near the base
- stiff rough hairs on both surfaces
- hairs/scales on the rachis
- small ultimate segments
- nearly continuous submarginal sori
- rolled margin forming the false indusium

The stiff basally swollen hairs are one of its strongest field characters.

## Purple cliffbrake
### Source name: Pellaea atropurpurea
### Current POWO: Hemionitis atropurpurea

This is probably the best **false-indusium teaching fern**.

Look for:
- shiny dark purple-black stipe and rachis
- dense curly hairs along the top of the rachis
- leathery blue-green segments
- fertile fronds longer/narrower than sterile fronds
- almost continuous brown sori around the underside margin
- rolled segment edge covering the sori

## Ovateleaf / zigzag cliffbrake
### Source name: Pellaea ovata
### Current POWO: Hemionitis ovata

The easiest clue is the **rachis**.

It bends sharply back and forth in a very obvious zigzag/flexuous pattern.

Then confirm:
- large 2–3-pinnate frond
- broad leathery ultimate segments
- recurved fertile margins
- marginal/submarginal sori

## Wright's cliffbrake
### Source name: Pellaea wrightiana
### Current POWO: Hemionitis wrightiana

Look for:
- dark shiny axes
- narrower oblong ultimate segments
- tiny mucronate tips
- marginal sori beneath recurved edges
- no dramatic zigzag rachis like P. ovata

## Taxonomy note

Current POWO has made a major lumping move here.

Our familiar Texas field-guide names:

- Cheilanthes horridula
- Pellaea atropurpurea
- Pellaea ovata
- Pellaea wrightiana

are currently treated by POWO as:

- Hemionitis aspera
- Hemionitis atropurpurea
- Hemionitis ovata
- Hemionitis wrightiana

The learner should show both names where useful because FNA, Texas fern literature, iNaturalist history and local references still commonly use Cheilanthes/Pellaea.

## Drought adaptation

These are not classic wet-forest ferns.

Many local members live in:
- limestone cracks
- cliff faces
- rocky slopes
- shallow moisture pockets

Useful xeric features include:
- leathery segments
- small segments
- rolled margins
- hairs/scales
- wiry dark axes
- protected rhizomes in rock cracks

Some xeric ferns also curl or contract strongly in drought and reopen after moisture returns.

Teach that as an **adaptation**, not as a required family character.

## Main fern-family comparison

### Pteridaceae
Marginal/submarginal sori; often false indusium; true indusium absent.

### Aspleniaceae
Elongated sori along veins with a true linear indusium.

### Dryopteridaceae
Often round sori with a true round/kidney-shaped indusium.

### Polypodiaceae
Often round naked sori set farther in from the margin.

## Best visual sequence

1. whole fern in rock crevice
2. frond
3. stipe + rachis
4. pinna + pinnule
5. flip frond over
6. sori
7. false indusium
8. sporangium + annulus
9. rough-lipfern hairs/scales
10. purple cliffbrake sori band
11. ovateleaf cliffbrake zigzag rachis
12. dry vs hydrated xeric fern
