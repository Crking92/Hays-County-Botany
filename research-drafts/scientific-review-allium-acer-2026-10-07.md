## Allium canadense versus Allium drummondii
LOOK HERE FIRST: inspect the spathe bracts at the base of the umbel, counting bracts and their veins on intact flowering material. The previous live Master Plant Pool review reports A. canadense generally 3–4 bracts with 3–7 veins versus A. drummondii generally 2–3 bracts with one vein each. Reconfirm numerical ranges against Flora of North America before presenting them as hard cutoffs. A. canadense may form aerial bulbils, but their absence does not identify A. drummondii. Flower color, leaf width, and pedicel length overlap. Needed matched photos: intact umbel with spathe; backlit close-up of bract venation; flower/bulbil arrangement with scale. Preserve taxonomy and target-zone range separately from learner admission.

## Acer grandidentatum versus Acer saccharinum
LOOK HERE FIRST: compare mature leaf sinus depth and the abaxial leaf surface. A. grandidentatum generally has broader lobes and shallower sinuses; A. saccharinum typically has very deep angular sinuses and a pale silvery underside. Confirm with matched mature leaf upper/lower surfaces, scaled sinus close-ups, and fruit/twig/bud views when available. Leaf variation means no single leaf silhouette should be treated as definitive. Historical A. saccharum infraspecific treatments of bigtooth maple must remain taxonomy references, not automatically merged into eastern sugar maple.

## 2026-10-07 authority cross-check and cautions

Flora of North America confirms the spathe distinction in the live Sheet: *Allium canadense* has 3–4 spathe bracts with 3–7 veins; *A. drummondii* has 2–3 with one vein each. It also gives canadense 0–60 flowers, pedicels 8–70 mm, and drummondii usually 10–25 flowers, pedicels 5–20 mm. Thus pedicel lengths and flower counts **overlap**. A UT Austin Plant Resources Center treatment describes a fused drummondii bud bract with 3–4 veins and three caudate tips: its bract unit/vein terminology may not match FNA's separated spathe-bract units. Flag this discrepancy for voucher-level examination before teaching an absolute vein-count key. Sources: https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=242101341 ; https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0000756137 ; https://w3.biosci.utexas.edu/prc/K12/pages/Allium%20drummondii.html .

For *Acer*, avoid teaching hairlessness versus pubescence as an absolute silver-maple split. Go Botany documents hairs on the underside of *A. saccharinum*, while the underside can also be glaucous/waxy; prioritize deep sinuses plus conspicuously pale undersides as a combination. *A. grandidentatum* is generally more shallowly lobed and may have short underside hairs. Mature leaf underside macros and paired same-scale blades are still needed. Sources: https://gobotany.nativeplanttrust.org/species/acer/saccharinum/ ; https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/acer-grandidentatum .
